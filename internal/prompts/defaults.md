# Generic defaults to avoid

Generated web design keeps landing in the same places. Each item below is legitimate for some briefs, but it appears whatever the subject, which makes it a default rather than a choice. When the brief pins down a direction, follow the brief, even when it asks for one of these. When the brief leaves an axis free, do not spend that freedom on any of these.

Maintain this list. Review it every month against the run history, and add any pattern that shows up in three or more of the last twenty runs.

## Palette

- Warm cream background (near #F4F1EA) with a high contrast serif display face and a terracotta or warm clay accent (near #D97757).
- Near black background (#0B0B0B, #111111) with one bright acid green or vermilion accent.
- Purple to blue gradient hero, or gradient washes used as decoration.
- Pure grey neutrals with no bias toward the accent hue.

## Type

- Inter or Space Grotesk as the safe default face.
- A monospace face for small data labels when nothing in the subject calls for it.
- Accenting one word or phrase in a headline with italic, bold or a different color.
- Tracked ALL CAPS labels, and an eyebrow label above every heading.

## Layout

- Everything centered.
- Content chopped into identical rounded cards, one radius on everything, the same soft grey shadow under each.
- Broadsheet layout with hairline rules, zero radius and dense newspaper columns, when the subject is not editorial.
- Hero made of a big number, a small label and a gradient accent.
- A hero sized to 100vh that pushes everything else below the first screen.

## Page chrome

- Numbered markers (01, 02, 03) on content that is not a sequence.
- Meta strings joined with middle dots ("A · B · C").
- Labels built as a word, a spaced dash and a fragment.
- An arrow appended to every link and button label.
- Emoji as section markers.

## Motion

- A fade and slide up entrance on every section.
- Hover transitions on every card.
- Content that stays invisible until a scroll observer fires.

## Copy

- Verbs such as unlock, elevate, transform, revolutionize, supercharge.
- Adjectives such as seamless, cutting edge, world class, next generation.
- Dash asides, "not X, but Y", a colon followed by a reveal, rhetorical questions as headings.
